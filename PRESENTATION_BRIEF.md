## SLIDE 11 — LIMITATIONS

**Headline:** What this analysis does not capture — and what more time would add

**Current limitations:**
- Grid cost is uniform at $25,000/km — terrain and road access penalties are not modelled (could add 20–30% in mountainous areas)
- No grid capacity constraints — assumes the existing network can absorb all new connections
- Demand is based on World Bank MTF standards, not actual household surveys in Benin
- Population data is WorldPop 2030 (modelled at 100m resolution) — not 2023 census micro-data
- SHS modelled as one product tier — does not capture the growing market for higher-tier systems

**With more time, I would add:**
- A willingness-to-pay module to check that least-cost solutions are actually affordable
- SBEE network capacity data to model real connection constraints
- Productive use demand (agriculture, water, commerce) to better represent economic activity
- Year-by-year cashflow modelling rather than a single 15-year total

## SLIDE 12 — WHAT THIS MEANS IN PRACTICE

**Headline:** Three different policy levers — not one uniform programme

**Grid now (8,864 settlements):**
Prioritise grid extension approval and financing for settlements where it is clearly least-cost even under wealth-adjusted demand. Focus on Atlantique, Zou, and southern Borgou where grid proximity makes this straightforward.

**Mini-grid pipeline (2,873 settlements):**

## SLIDE 4 — POPULATION ENRICHMENT (WorldPop 2030)

**Headline:** Static population understates future demand — projected to 2030

```python
# enrich_worldpop.py
stats = rasterstats.zonal_stats(
    gdf, WORLDPOP_TIF, stats=["sum"], all_touched=True
)
# all_touched=True is essential — tiny polygons return empty without it

# Fallback for small settlements with poor raster coverage
raw_gf = pop_2030_series / base_pop_series
suspect = raw_gf < 0.85    # less than 85% of expected population captured

for dept in df["admin1_name"].unique():
    dept_gf = raw_gf[dept_mask & ~suspect].median()
    df.loc[dept_mask & suspect, "pop_2030"] = df.loc[...,"pop_base"] * dept_gf
```

**Why this matters:**
Population feeds directly into: annual demand, capital cost per connection, urban/rural classification, and OnSSET's prioritisation order. A settlement of 200 people today that grows to 320 by 2033 can cross the mini-grid viability threshold — getting this wrong means wrong technology for that community.

**Coverage achieved:** 99.8% of 17,175 settlements.

## SLIDE 5 — THE CRITICAL FIX: HV LINE DISTANCE

**Headline:** GridDistCurrent = min(substation, HV line) — not substation alone

**The problem:**
```
Settlement A:  40 km to substation, 5 km to HV line
Default model: GridDistCurrent = 40 km  →  grid appears expensive
Corrected:     GridDistCurrent = 5 km   →  grid is cheap, correct
```

**The fix:**
```python
# prepare_onsset_input.py
_sub = pd.to_numeric(df["dist_to_substations"], errors="coerce")
_hvl = pd.to_numeric(df["dist_to_hvline_km"],   errors="coerce")

out["GridDistCurrent"]   = np.minimum(_sub, _hvl).round(3)
out["CurrentHVLineDist"] = df["dist_to_hvline_km"].round(3)
```

**The numbers:**
- 8,981 settlements are closer to an HV line than to a substation
- Mean `GridDistCurrent`: 25.5 km → **19.9 km** after correction
- This is the single most impactful change in the entire pipeline

**Rationale:**
In practice, engineers connect settlements to HV corridors by installing a step-down transformer at the tapping point. It is standard practice. A model that ignores this overstates grid cost for nearly half of Benin's settlements.

## SLIDE 6 — DEMAND MODEL: THE MTF FRAMEWORK

**Headline:** Used the World Bank Multi-Tier Framework as the demand standard

```
Annual demand [kWh/HH/year] = MTF_KWH[tier]

MTF_KWH = {
    1:    4.5   — basic lighting only
    2:   73.0   — lighting + phone + fan  ← rural baseline
    3:  365.0   — lighting + TV + productive use  ← urban
    4: 1,250.0
    5: 3,000.0
}

Households = Population ÷ 5.2
(Benin DHS 2017-18 national average household size)
```

**Demand growth:**
Linear ramp from the current 42% electrification rate (World Bank 2023) to 95% by 2041, with an intermediate target of 70% by 2033. This is the standard OnSSET two-step approach for West Africa planning.

**Why Tier 2 for rural baseline:**
Tier 2 (73 kWh/HH/year) is the minimum meaningful electrification level — it covers lighting, phone charging, and a small fan. It is the internationally accepted benchmark for basic access. Using Tier 1 would understate demand; using Tier 3 would overstate it and artificially favour grid.

## SLIDE 7 — EXTENSION 1: WEALTH-DISAGGREGATED DEMAND

**Headline:** OnSSET modified to assign demand by wealth quartile, not uniformly

**The problem with OnSSET's default:**
`prepare_wtf_tier_columns()` sets identical demand targets across all rural settlements. A poor hamlet in Atacora and a prosperous market town in Atlantique both receive 73 kWh/HH/year. That is not realistic.

**Extension (`src/onsset_extensions.py`):**
```python
# Multipliers derived from actual MTF tier ratios — not arbitrary
_MTF = {1: 38.7, 2: 219.0, 3: 803.0}   # kWh/HH/year (OnSSET internal units)

MULTIPLIER = {
    "q1": 38.7  / 219.0,   # = 0.177  poorest 25%  → Tier 1
    "q2": 1.0,             # unchanged
    "q3": 1.0,             # unchanged
    "q4": 803.0 / 219.0,  # = 3.669  richest 25%   → Tier 3
}

def apply_wealth_tier_shift(sp_df):
    q1_thresh = sp_df["mean_rwi"].quantile(0.25)
    q3_thresh = sp_df["mean_rwi"].quantile(0.75)
    for col in tier_cols:             # ResidentialDemandTier1..5
        for idx, row in sp_df.iterrows():
            if row["IsUrban"] == 2:
                continue              # exclude large cities (RWI resolution issue)
            q = wealth_quartile(row["mean_rwi"], q1_thresh, q3_thresh)
            sp_df.at[idx, col] *= MULTIPLIER[q]
```

**Why 0.177 and 3.669 specifically:**
These are not tuning parameters. They are the exact ratios between MTF Tier 1 and Tier 2, and between Tier 3 and Tier 2, as defined in the World Bank's own framework. The adjustment encodes the judgment that "a Q1 settlement needs Tier 1 service" using the MTF's own consumption ratios.

**Why exclude IsUrban = 2:**
RWI at 2.4 km resolution averages across a city's mixed wealth levels. Applying a 3.7× multiplier to a city of 500,000+ people produces implausible billion-dollar CAPEX. The extension is designed for rural settlement scale.

**Hook point:**
The extension runs after `prepare_wtf_tier_columns()` (so the tier columns exist) and before `set_scenario_variables()` (which reads them). The order is critical — getting it wrong silently produces zero effect.

## SLIDE 8 — EXTENSION 2: SITE-SPECIFIC SOLAR GHI

**Headline:** National average GHI replaced with a settlement-level solar resource

```python
# enrich_solar.py
stats = rasterstats.zonal_stats(
    settlements_gdf, PVGIS_GHI_TIF,
    stats=["mean"], all_touched=True
)
df["ghi_kwh_m2_day"] = [s["mean"] or NATIONAL_FALLBACK for s in stats]
```

**Why it matters:**
Mini-grid system capacity is sized proportionally to GHI. A 10% difference in solar resource translates to ~10% difference in mini-grid LCOE. Alibori (5.5 kWh/m²/day) has measurably better solar than Mono (4.9 kWh/m²/day). Using a national average of 5.1 for both artificially equalises their economics.

**Effect on results:**
Northern departments see ~8–12% lower mini-grid LCOE than the national-average model would suggest. This contributes to the disproportionate mini-grid concentration in Alibori and Atacora in the extended scenario.

## SLIDE 10 — TECHNOLOGY COST MODEL

**Headline:** LCOE-based comparison — same formula applied consistently to all three technologies

**LCOE formula:**
```
LCOE = (CAPEX × CRF + OPEX_annual + Running_cost_annual) ÷ kWh_generated_annual

CRF = r(1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1)   [Capital Recovery Factor]
      where r = 8% discount rate, n = technology lifetime
```

| Parameter | Grid Extension | Mini-Grid PV | Solar Home System |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAPEX | $25,000/km MV + $375/HH LV | $2,900/kW all-in | $350/system |
| OPEX | 3%/year | 4%/year | $25/HH/year |
| Losses | 18% T&D | 5% distribution | — |
| Lifetime | 30 years | 20 years | 10 years |
| Running cost | $0.12/kWh wholesale (CEB 2024) | — | — |

**Why these specific numbers:**
- Grid $25,000/km: mid-range of AfDB West Africa appraisal data ($20,000–$30,000/km)
- MG $2,900/kW: midpoint of GEP West Africa project database ($2,500–$3,200/kW, all-in)
- SHS $350: GOGLA 2023 market data for quality-verified Tier 2 system
- 8% discount rate: World Bank standard for SSA infrastructure project analysis
- $0.12/kWh: CEB published bulk tariff (what SBEE pays for power, not retail tariff)
